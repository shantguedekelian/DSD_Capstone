A Recommendation System for Permit Submitters

DSC 180 · Domain D42 · Week 5 update

Shant · Alyx · Adit

1 · Problem statement

Background: what DSD is and what the problem is

What DSD is

The City of San Diego's Development Services Department (DSD) reviews plans and issues permits for construction and development projects, then inspects the work.

The problem

Resubmittal cycles are high. Applicants submit, get comments back, fix things, and resubmit, and every round adds more review time for the City and more waiting for the applicant.

2 · Current system

What the system is like today and where it breaks

  • People have no clear idea what is needed for their permit to get approved.
  • The information they need is too spread out for a normal person to know all of it.
  • That leads to many resubmittals, and money and time spent getting nowhere.
  • Applicants get frustrated, and the City wastes money and resources on repeat reviews.
Early evidence from the Reviews data (preliminary): of 168,278 finished record-and-discipline reviews, 53.9% needed at least one resubmittal. Only 46% were approved in a single round, and about 5% took 5 or more rounds.
Earlier exploratory work: permits that need plan review take about 100 days, and frequent filers get building permits in about 20 days versus about 100 for everyone else. Experience seems to matter, which fits the idea that knowing what to include is the hard part.

3 · Our solution

A recommendation system for submitters

A tool that helps people prepare for their specific type of permit, so they know what to expect before they submit.

Risk-based tips for the submitter

How we'll build it

  • Train an ML model on the review data we received. For each record and discipline, the target is the number of review rounds it took (or simply: did it need a resubmittal?).
  • Look for patterns by permit type and by location.
  • Test the model on records it has never seen, split by record so the same permit never appears in both training and testing.
  • We are still exploring, but we are currently leaning toward a decision tree (a decision model).

What we can recommend today vs. what we need

What our data supports now: risk by reviewer. For example: "Permits like yours had about a 70% chance of a Structural resubmittal, so be prepared for that." We can do this because the Reviews data tells us the outcome of every review (Recheck Required or Signed Off), by permit type, location and discipline.
What it can't support yet: "you're missing X." We don't have the reviewers' comments, the reasons for corrections, or the list of documents that were submitted. We only know that a review needed a recheck, not why. Tips about specific missing items need that data.
How we get it:
  • Ask the DSD team to share the review comments and correction reasons.
  • If they can't, collect them ourselves by web scraping, but only if the comments are publicly available and allowed to be collected. We would check with DSD first.

4 · Impact

Why this matters

  • Reduces friction on both sides: easier for submitters and applicants, and less time and money spent by both the applicant and the City.
  • Faster turnaround, not just for this permit but for others too, because fewer repeat reviews free up reviewers.
  • A speedup in the overall permitting system.
  • As we saw, frequent filers get building permits in about 20 days versus about 100 for everyone else. If we can get people closer to the 20 day waiting period that would be a significant improvement.

5 · Progress so far

Reviews data: what we built and what we found

We turned the Reviews data into one row per record and discipline, with the number of review rounds as our target.

  • Discipline: a specialty reviewer, such as Structural, Electrical or Fire.
  • Cycle: one round of review. The first review is cycle 1, and each resubmittal adds one.

What we found (finished records only)

  • 53.9% of record-discipline reviews needed at least one resubmittal.
  • It varies a lot by discipline: Minor Right of Way is at about 91%, Engineering Building Review, Structural, Water & Sewer and Right of Way Dry Utilities are around 70–75%, and Electrical and Fire Alarm are around 33%.
  • Faster processing tracks (Express, Expedite) look worse in the raw numbers, but that is mostly because they handle different kinds of work. Within Building Construction, Express and Standard are both 65.2%.

Open question for DSD: does a discipline's cycle number go up only when that discipline asks for corrections, or whenever the whole record is resubmitted? The answer decides whether our target measures each discipline's own corrections.
